                                      6. ---
                                     
                                      7. ### Full Draft
                                     
                                      8. ---
                                     
                                      9. I didn't plan any of this.
                                     
                                      10. It started with five Chrome tabs and a frustrated business owner — me — clicking "Allow" over and over again while an AI agent tried to set up a Meta ad campaign.
                                     
                                      11. The agent was doing its job. I was doing mine. The problem was that my job had become: sitting there pressing Confirm.
                                     
                                      12. ---
                                     
                                      13. **The setup**
                                     
                                      14. I run a small catering business. Not a tech startup. Not a dev shop. A catering business — events, menus, quotes, client coordination.
                                     
                                      15. A few months ago I started using Claude for Chrome to help run operations. One tab for SEO. One for ads. One for inventory. One for client replies. One for quotes.
                                     
                                      16. Five tabs. Five agents. One me.
                                     
                                      17. The idea was: I hand off the repetitive stuff to AI, and I focus on the things only I can do.
                                     
                                      18. What actually happened: I became the permission department.
                                     
                                      19. Every action the agent wanted to take — fill a form, click a button, update a field — needed a human to authorize it. So I'd sit there, watching the agent work, pressing Confirm every thirty seconds.
                                     
                                      20. I wasn't doing less work. I was doing different work. Less thinking, more clicking.
                                     
                                      21. ---
                                     
                                      22. **The question that changed everything**
                                     
                                      23. At some point I got annoyed enough to ask out loud: *Is there someone who could do the confirming for me?*
                                     
                                      24. The answer — obvious in retrospect — was yes. Another agent.
                                     
                                      25. I had a second computer sitting unused. I set up Claude for Chrome on it, connected it to the first machine via Chrome Remote Desktop, and suddenly I had an agent whose only job was to watch the other agents and press Confirm on their behalf.
                                     
                                      26. I called it the sixth shrimp. (Long story. The other five agents were already the shrimp.)
                                     
                                      27. The sixth shrimp wasn't smart. It wasn't strategic. It was just always watching, always available, and willing to click things I didn't want to click.
                                     
                                      28. That felt like a win.
                                     
                                      29. ---
                                     
                                      30. **What I actually learned**
                                     
                                      31. The technical setup matters less than the questions you ask.
                                     
                                      32. When an agent gets stuck, I've learned to ask: *What do you need? Can you get it yourself? What's the slowest part?*
                                     
                                      33. Not "what's the technical solution" — I don't always understand those. But I can always ask: *is there a dumber, faster way to do this?*
                                     
                                      34. Half the time the answer is yes.
                                     
                                      35. Sometimes an agent will explain that it needs an MCP integration to proceed. I'll ask: *what's actually blocked right now — can't read data, or can't click the button?* And then: *can you screenshot it and work from there?*
                                     
                                      36. Often: yes.
                                     
                                      37. The non-engineer advantage is that I don't have a preference for elegant solutions. I just want it to work.
                                     
                                      38. ---
                                     
                                      39. **What's next**
                                     
                                      40. The system works. Mostly.
                                     
                                      41. The parts that still break: Meta Ads and Google Ads. Too many permissions, too many dynamic fields, too many things that change between sessions.
                                     
                                      42. The parts that run cleanly: WordPress SEO, quote drafts, client message templates.
                                     
                                      43. I'm documenting all of it here — not as a tutorial, but as a running log of what breaks and what holds.
                                     
                                      44. Next post: what happens when an agent does something wrong, and you're not watching.
